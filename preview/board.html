The four columns are not four statuses — they are a mapping over fourteen. The stage the app stores is Inward, Production, Flight Test and so on; statusCategory() folds all fourteen into these four buckets, and the strip of count tiles on your list screen is already counting them. So this board is buildable with no new field, no backend change and no migration: it is a second drawing of a number the app already sums. What it is not is informative — Open is 37 IRs that are at ten different real stages, and a column that wide tells you nothing you did not know.

This board needs one small thing that does not exist yet. statusCategory() maps fourteen stages onto four buckets. Mapping them onto the six sections instead is the table above — about fifteen lines of pure function, no backend, no new field, no write. The seven columns add up to the Open tile from the first board and no more: the other 375 IRs are off the board entirely, because a board shows work in progress and an IR that is Delivered is not in progress. A board that looked like it held everything would be a lie you could not see.

IR-409 slides to E the moment D is saved. Three things go wrong with it. done[] only records saved, never finished — a section saved with three empty fields moves the card just as far as a completed one. The stage and the sections drift apart the first time somebody works on F before finishing E, which your engineers will do. And statusAt is overwritten on every save, so "In status 3d" becomes permanently 0d and the overdue limits stop meaning anything.

It advances, and the person who knows better can drag it back. The catch is that a drag is a write, and every drag would need to say whether it moved the clock. The shape that survives: saving a section proposes a move, and only a hand-set change writes statusAt — so the board stays current without the ageing clock becoming fiction. That is rule 1's guarantee kept, with rule 2's freshness bought on top.

The app has already answered two of these three, in its own source. From the comment on ownedStatus(): "saving Section B is not triage, so an IR whose Section B was saved must go on following the Sheet's Col D until somebody changes the status here." And from syncIRStateAfterSectionSave(): "Status is no longer mirrored from a section form… A section save that happens to post a status key must not be able to move the workflow clock." Both sentences exist because someone already tried rule 2 and took it out. The reason is in the third column of the table above: statusAt.
Where the board goes

A switch beside the filters, not a seventh tab

The board is another view of the list you already have, so it reads the same rows, the same search box and the same filters — a separate "Board" tab would mean filtering twice and finding different numbers on each. One switch on the List screen, and both views obey whatever is typed above them.

What this is, and what it is not

The honest list

It is a drawing of data you haveNo migration

Every card on this page is built from fields the app already stores: ir.status, the assignee, the category, the raised date, and ir.done[]. Adopting a board is a rendering pass, not a data change.

Dragging needs a write per dragDecide

A drag that persists is a store write and an audit entry. That is fine — the app already writes on every triage save — but it is a decision about who may drag, and it is the TR access axis, not a new one.

The board is inertBy rule

Nothing on this page can be dragged, clicked or scrolled by script — the review pages are forbidden a <script> and a test enforces it. The third rule above is a picture of a drag, not a drag.

No subtasks, no swimlanes, no WIP limitsNot shown

Real kanban tools add cards inside cards and cap how many may sit in a column. Neither maps onto anything the app stores, so neither is drawn. If you want one, say which.

On your phone it is one column at a timeSideways

A board is wide by nature. On the phone it scrolls sideways inside its own frame — the page itself never scrolls sideways — so you see one column and the edge of the next. That is the honest cost of a board on a 390px screen, and it is why board A may be the better phone answer even though board B is the better one.
