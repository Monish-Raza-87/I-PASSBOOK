What it is

A light grey canvas with white cards floating on it, gentle shadows, wide corner radii and a tinted primary button. It is the visual language of every well-made product tool, which means nobody has to learn it — and it makes a form-heavy passbook feel lighter than it is.

Why you might pick it

  • Instantly familiar. Nothing on screen asks to be learnt.
  • Cards separate the passbook sections clearly, which suits a nine-section form
  • The softest of the three on a small screen — big tap targets, nothing cramped
  • Ages well and looks deliberate rather than inherited

What it costs you

  • The least dense. Noticeably fewer IRs per phone screen, and this list is 412 rows.
  • The shadow-and-card look is everywhere, so it makes the app look like a product, not like Indrones
  • Two competing ideas meet here: a heavy data list wants density and a SaaS card wants air

The fine print

  • All four pages carry identical markup, taken from the app's real class names, and colour from its real tokens. Only the styling differs — in Option D that styling is mostly arrangement.
  • They are static. Adopting one is a separate pass through the app; that pass is the expensive part, which is why you are choosing first.
  • Sample records are shown (IR-412 and friends). The statuses, categories and REPAIR sub-categories are the real ones.
