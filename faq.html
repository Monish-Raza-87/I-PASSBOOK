Getting in

How do I get an account?

An administrator creates it for you. There is no sign-up screen and no way to request one from inside the app — an account is made, and its details are handed to you.

It is my first time. What will I see?

You sign in with the temporary password you were given, and the app asks you to set a password of your own before it lets you any further. Until you do, the app is presentation only — it holds no session for you and there is nothing behind that screen to reach.

Do I type a password every morning?

No. Day to day you give your email address, the app emails you a six-digit code, and you type that. The password door is still there if you would rather use it.

Can I unlock faster than that?

On a device you have already signed in on, yes — with your fingerprint, or with a pattern. This is a local unlock on that device and it is set up from inside the app once you are already signed in.

Can I stay signed in on my laptop and my phone at once?

No, and this is deliberate. One device stays signed in per account: signing in on your phone signs the earlier device out. If you have been signed out somewhere you did not expect, this is almost always why.

Codes & passwords

How long does the emailed code last?

Eight and a half hours from the moment it is emailed — a working day. The clock starts when the mail is sent, so a code requested at 2pm is good until 10:30pm. Within that window the same code works for every sign-in; you do not need a fresh one each time.

Someone asked me for my code. Should I give it?

Never share it

A sign-in code is the same thing as your signature. Nobody at the service desk needs it, and nobody should ever ask for it. If you get such a request, report it.

I typed the code wrong a few times.

The code burns itself out after five wrong attempts, whatever time is left on it. Request a new one. Repeated wrong attempts also lock that email's door for fifteen minutes — if you are certain the code is right and it still refuses, wait a quarter of an hour rather than trying again and again.

I have forgotten my password.

Use the reset link on the sign-in screen. If you normally sign in with a code instead, you can simply keep doing that — the password is an optional door, not the front one.

I did not try to sign in, but I got a code.

Then someone else is trying to get in with your email. Tell an administrator straight away. Do not forward the code to anyone, including the person who asked.

Finding an IR

How do I find one ticket?

Type into the search box on the IR list — it matches the IR number and the drone ID. The filters narrow the same list by year, month, status, category and customer.

What is the board view?

The IR list has a List / Board switch. The board shows the same tickets as cards in columns, one column per stage of the job. It is the same list seen sideways, so the search box and every filter apply to both — switch between them without losing what you had typed.

What is Legacy Records?

The old workbook, shown read-only in a frame. It is there so you can still reach work that predates the app, and it stays current alongside it. Nothing you do in that frame changes the app.

Where is the site?

On the ticket, the site location is written out as text with a link beside it. Tapping the link opens that place in Google Maps. Nothing is loaded from Google — and no location leaves your device — until you tap it.

Working a ticket

What do the sections mean?

A ticket has an Overview and six numbered sections — B inward checklist, C IQC visual inspection, D investigation, E production/rework, F quality test, G PDI and dispatch. Each section is a form with its own Save.

"3 of 6 sections saved" — does that mean the ticket is finished?

No, and the wording is careful about it. The count records that a section's Save button was pressed, not that the section was filled in fully. A section saved once stays counted, so treat the number as progress, never as a completion certificate.

What moves a ticket forward?

A person does, deliberately. Saving a section never moves the workflow on by itself — no status changes behind your back, and no clock starts on its own. The status changes when someone sets it in Triage, or presses the move button that appears on a card on the board.

What does the status mean?

The status is the stage of the job, from Open and Remote Support through Inward, Visual Inspection, QC Investigation, Production, QC and Flight Test to PDI, Approval, Delivered and Close. Hold and Other are outside that run. On the board these are grouped into columns, so "Not started" covers Open and Remote Support and "Finished" covers Delivered, Close and Other.

When is a ticket counted as late?

It depends on priority, counted from the last time its status changed:

  • Urgent — more than 1 day
  • High — more than 3 days
  • Medium — more than 7 days
  • Low — more than 14 days

A ticket with no priority is treated as 14 days. Only tickets that are actually still open can be late — a Delivered or Closed ticket is never flagged, however long it sat there.

What does the number beside an assignee mean?

Who the ticket is with. The IR list shows it next to the IR number. "Unassigned" means nobody has been given it yet, and it is a real state, not a gap to ignore.

Reading the charts

What is on the Insights screen?

Three headline figures — raised, open now, and late now — then how many were raised in each of the last twelve months, the spread of statuses, how REPAIR tickets break down by sub-category, and a card per person with how many they have open and how many are late.

The chart total does not match the list. Which is wrong?

Neither, usually — they are answering different questions. The charts read every ticket; the list reads what your filters and search have left. Clear the filters and the two agree.

Why is there a "No date" bar?

Because some tickets genuinely have no date on them. Leaving them out would make the bars add up to less than the total printed above them, and a chart that silently disagrees with its own headline is worse than one that admits the gap.

Why is there an "Unassigned" row under People?

For the same reason. It is shown even when it is zero, so the numbers in that card always add up to the total rather than quietly leaving out whoever nobody has picked up yet.

Does it show departments?

No. An IR does not carry a department, so a department chart would be inventing one. It counts assignees, because that is what the data actually has.

Who can edit what

Can I see everything?

Everyone signed in can read and comment on every ticket and its six sections, and the Overview. That is the same for all accounts.

Then what is restricted?

Editing. Whether you can change a section is set per account, per department, and a section can be given to more than one department so that more than one team can work on it.

I can read a section but the Save button will not work.

Then that section has not been granted to a department you belong to. It is yours to read and comment on, not to change. Ask an administrator if you should have it.

Who can set the status and priority?

Triage — Customer Relations and Management. It is a separate permission from section editing: a person can be able to work a section without being able to change the stage the ticket is at.

The Log Analyser

What does it do?

It reads an ArduPilot flight log from a drone's removable card and reports what is in it — the flight, the vibration and GPS quality, and any errors the flight controller recorded.

Does the log file get uploaded anywhere?

No

The file is opened and read entirely inside your browser, on your own device. The log never leaves the machine you opened it on, and nothing about it is sent to the service desk or anyone else.

Can I keep the result with the ticket?

Yes. You can push the report onto the investigation section of an IR so the finding sits with the ticket rather than in a file on one laptop.

The numbers it quotes — where do they come from?

The thresholds are ArduPilot's own published guidance, not this app's opinion. The analyser reads the log and applies those numbers; it is a reader, not a judge, and it will not tell you a flight was fine.

Reporting a problem

I do not have an account. Can I report a fault?

Yes. On the sign-in screen there is a Report a problem entry that needs no account at all. It opens the problem-reporting form the service desk already uses, and the form can also be opened in a new tab if the window gives you any trouble.

What can I see from there?

The form, and this page. Nothing else. That entry reads no ticket, no customer name and no IR number — it is a way in, not a window. Everything else in the app is behind a sign-in.

Who receives what I send?

It goes to the service desk exactly as it does today, through the same form. The form is hosted by Google; nothing is loaded from Google until you open that window.

Your data and your device

Where does the app keep the records?

In a store in the organisation's own Google Drive, reached through the service desk's own backend. It is not a hosted product and no third-party service holds your tickets.

What leaves my device?

Only what the app needs to do its job: your sign-in, and the saves you press. Flight logs stay on your device. Nothing is sent to Google for a map until you tap the map link, and nothing is loaded from the problem-reporting form until you open it.

Is the app watching me?

No. There is no analytics, no tracking and no advertising in it. It asks for a camera or a location only where a feature genuinely needs one, and it tells you when it does.

I am on a phone with a poor signal.

The app is built to keep working from what it last loaded. What it cannot promise is fresh data: a save needs the connection to reach the store, and a screen you left open can show an older copy than the one on the server.

When something looks wrong

The first sign-in of the day took half a minute.

That is the backend waking up, not you being slow or the app being broken. It sleeps after a few minutes of quiet and takes a good while to come back the first time; after that it answers in a second or two. It is kept awake during working hours to make this rarer, but it can still happen.

A save said "Not saved — press again".

The write did not reach the store, and the app is telling you rather than pretending. Press Save again. If it keeps happening, the connection is the problem — check it before you re-type anything.

Someone else's change is missing from my screen.

Then your screen is showing what it loaded earlier. Reload the page: a page that is already open does not quietly pull in a newer copy of itself.

A banner said there is a new version. What do I do?

Tap it, or reload. The app tells you when a newer build exists rather than swapping itself out under your hands mid-form. One refresh moves you onto it, and after that the app's own updates are automatic.

I have set the app up on my phone. Does it open full screen?

Yes — it can be installed to your home screen and opens without browser furniture, like an app rather than a web page.

None of this helped.

Tell the service desk. Say which ticket you were on, which section, and what you expected instead — that is enough for them to find it.
