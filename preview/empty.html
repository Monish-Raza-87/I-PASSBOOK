The honest summary: almost nothing genuinely open in the illustration world is industrial. The three licence-bulletproof sets are all hand-drawn PEOPLE — a doodle of somebody unboxing something, a peep waving, a figure in a navy jumper. None of them is a drone part, a checklist or a telemetry trace. If that warmth is what you want at "no IRs found", they are the safest way to get it and the licence is beyond question. If what you want is a picture that belongs to this app's own language, the third section is the only one that qualifies — and it costs about 1 KB instead of 87.
The app's own line language, enlarged

Ours, drawn the way every glyph in the app is already drawn

Every icon in the app comes from one table, ICON_PATHS: 24x24, fill:none, stroke:currentColor, round caps and joins. These are the same drawing at 96px in the same idiom, so there is nothing to keep in sync — no vendored file, no licence file, and no override needed for any of the four accent presets or either theme. The accent stroke is var(--accent), which is what makes that true.

What this costs, and what it is not. One 96x96 line drawing is about 1 KB, it is written with our tokens, and it needs no licence because we drew it. What it is not is warm — it is an icon at illustration size, and it will read as a diagram, not as a person. If the point of an empty state is to be friendly, this is the wrong answer and it says so.
Open Doodles — CC0, 33 ready-made doodles

The pink one, and the same artwork pointed at our tokens

This is the whole finding on one screen. Every one of Open Doodles' 33 files uses exactly two fills: #000000 for the line and #FF5678 for the accent. The first card is the file as shipped. The second is the same markup with two CSS rules mapping those two values to var(--ink-gray-9) and var(--accent) — a presentation attribute loses to any CSS rule, so this is a real re-colour, not a filter.

Read the register, not the drawing. Twenty-nine of the thirty-three are people doing something leisurely — ballet, bikini, moshing, roller-skating, ice-cream, zombieing. They are also large: 21 KB to 200 KB each, against about 1 KB for ours.
Does it actually follow the palette?

One artwork, four presets, no overrides

The claim above, tested rather than asserted. The same re-coloured doodle under each of the app's four accent presets — Blue, Violet, Teal, Graphite. Nothing changes between the tiles except a data-palette attribute, because the accent role is a custom property and custom properties inherit. An as-shipped doodle would be pink in all four.

Open Peeps and Humaaans — also CC0

The two that cannot be re-coloured by a rule

Both are equally free and equally safe to commit. The difference is that their artwork carries its own palette — Peeps uses six fills, this Humaaans figure eleven — so the fix that works for Open Doodles does not exist here. Making either follow var(--accent) means redrawing it, which is not a licence question any more.

Two things worth knowing before you look. Both sets are part-kits more than picture-books — Humaaans is a library of heads, torsos and legs you compose a figure from, and what is rendered here is one of the artist's own ready-made examples. And both are portrait figures with faces, which is a bigger commitment than a doodle: a face in an error state is a mascot whether it was meant to be one or not.
Can we even use it?

The question is not "is it free" — it is "may it be committed"

This repository is public, so putting an SVG in it is redistribution, not use. That single test removes most of the illustration world, including the set everyone recommends first.

Open DoodlesCC0 — committable

Public domain, no conditions at all. "Free for Commercial and Personal Use. No need to credit, license, or anything." 33 ready-made doodles; two fills each.

Open PeepsCC0 — committable

Public domain under CC0. Hand-drawn people, largely a part-kit; six fills.

HumaaansCC0 — committable

Public domain under CC0. Mix-and-match vector people; eleven fills.

Tabler IllustrationsNo — paid, and restricted

A codecalm.net product whose licence forbids use in open-source or freely-available products. This repository is both. Tabler's CSS and icons are MIT and were read for their measured geometry; the illustrations cannot be used at all.

unDrawNo — and it is the trap

The most-recommended free set on the web, promising no attribution, while its terms forbid distributing the assets in packs — which is exactly what vendoring them here is. It would have been the obvious first pick on the FAQ headline alone.

Storyset, Blush, DrawKit, ManyPixels, absurd.designNo

Bespoke licences, variously restrictive; one of them is non-commercial.

If one is chosen. Three of its SVGs get written into the app in the same place every other glyph lives — the ICON_PATHS idiom — with var(--accent) in place of the baked-in colour, and the set's copyright line goes into vendor/ beside the file, the way pdf-lib's already does. If none is chosen, the five files under vendor/illustrations/ are deleted and the board with them; that is a two-line change.
