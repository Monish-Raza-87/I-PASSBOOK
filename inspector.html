R-05aBlock

Inward rows. The Inward checklist has eleven fixed rows. When you close Section B, every row must carry an answer — either the part with its quantity, or "DID NOT COME".

A row left untouched is the single most common way a checklist gets closed without being done. Two honest notes: three rows (Tripod/Bipod, Center Pole, Toolkit-Box And Accessories) have no DID NOT COME option today and will get one; and one row is a free-text field, so "any text" counts.

R-05cBlock

Repair table. The Particulars For Repair table is the one table you build row by row. When you close that section, every row you have added must carry a part name, a quantity and a rate.

An empty table is fine — it means no repair costs. It is a half-written row that is the problem. This is the same rule as R-130, seen from the table's side.

R-05dNot a rule

The dispatch checklist needs no rule. It is derived from Section B — the rows appear because of what was recorded in Inward, not because someone typed them. Enforcing it twice would only create a second way to fail.

Q4 · How far should maturity go?

You asked the right question, and my first answer was too timid. You asked why it should not learn technical things, learn investigations, and become another arm of CR and QC. It should. The line is not about how much it learns — it is about who decides.

What it learns: your vocabulary and phrasing · model codes, part names, couriers · what a normal investigation paragraph looks like · and the technical associations in your own history — which symptom has led to which root cause, on which model. Over time that is a genuine knowledge base of how Indrones drones actually fail. It is built only from your own IRs.

What it does with it: it suggests. "On three earlier S25P IRs this symptom traced to the ESC — here is the IR number." A Note with a source, never a verdict. That is a real second arm for QC, because it points at the record that already answers the question; and a real second arm for CR, because it can see the pattern before a customer does.

The line: it never changes a rule by itself. When it comes to believe something should be a rule, it proposes it to you — and an unticked rule is not a rule. A helper that quietly relaxes its own standards would be worse than having none.

Decided — no outsourced model. Your rule: "data privacy is must… not an outsourced model which sends my data out." So nothing about an IR ever leaves Indrones. What that leaves is worth being exact about, because "a model inside my app" can mean three different things:

  • Retrieval — buildable now, and it is most of the value. Every past IR indexed by model, symptom and root cause, so the Inspector can answer "three earlier S25P IRs had this symptom and they traced to the ESC — here are the numbers." No model, no data leaving, and it is a genuine second arm for QC.
  • A small model running inside the browser — possible in principle, not on your phones. So what I will build is the first one: retrieval and templates, with the source IR numbers attached. It will say something true and traceable rather than something confident and unverifiable — which for a compliance record is the only kind of answer worth having.

Q5 · Auto-save — Confirmed

Agreed, and this is what gets built first. The Save button goes; every change is recorded as you make it. Two buttons where one is enough is exactly the confusion you describe, and closing is the only moment the app truly changes state. Four conditions I will hold to, because without them it is worse than a button:

  • "Saved" must mean saved. If the phone is offline or the session has expired, the indicator says so and keeps retrying. A false "Saved" loses work and destroys trust in every other indicator in the app.
  • Flush before it can be lost. Save on a short pause in typing, then again when the field loses focus, when the tab is hidden, or when you leave the section. A phone locking mid-sentence must not lose the sentence.
  • Not one write per keystroke. The Drive store costs per operation, not per byte, so saving on every letter would multiply what the app costs to run. A short debounce, plus the flush points above.
  • It must tell you what happened. A small, quiet "Saved just now" that appears and fades — the same discipline as the toast, which replaces rather than queues.

Confirming this also settles something quietly. Today the app records a section as "done" the moment it is saved. With auto-save that becomes meaningless — which is exactly what we want, because "done" finally gets to mean what it says.

Q6 · The Postmaster — Decided

Your ruling: every 2 hours, one collected email. Real risk goes immediately. That is what gets built. This is the one part of the Inspector that can annoy people who have done nothing wrong, so the shape below is where the anti-spam promise actually lives.

The app already has a notification system — nudges, an unread badge, a bell, a panel, and one email per assignment. The Postmaster feeds that rather than building a second channel beside it. One place decides who hears what, and one place to calm down if it turns out too loud.

How it sends

  • Every 2 hours, everything collected into one email. One message, not one per finding — so a bad morning is still one email, not nine.
  • Nothing to report means no email at all. The clock only sends when there is something new in it. Quiet days stay quiet, which is what keeps the loud ones read.
  • Blocks and Asks are never emailed, ever. They are instructions to the person standing in front of the field. An email about a block that was fixed an hour ago is pure noise.

The "real risk" list — yours to hold, not mine to fix

Your ruling: the list stays open, and it sits on CR's desk. Not a list I bake into the code — a setting CR can change without anyone touching the app. CR decides which rules go out at once and which wait for the collected email, and CR decides who receives them.

  • Seeded with six suggestions, the ones I proposed, so the setting is not blank on day one. CR can move any rule on or off, and add rules I have not thought of.
  • Recipients default to CR — which already includes Management, since Ravi Singh is both. That is my recommendation, and CR can change it.
  • Nobody is added to a recipient list silently. A person only starts receiving mail if CR puts them there.

The six seeded suggestions: R-131 / R-132 — a section marked complete that is empty, or half filled. R-134 — a dispatch with no PDI report. R-103 / R-104 — a zero quantity or cost. R-105 — a future date on a past event. R-109 — two serials in one IR. R-135 — a closed IR reopened. All six are a record that cannot be trusted, which is why they are the starting set rather than a person who merely forgot something.

What I am taking as agreed, since you did not say otherwise

  • Silence when there is nothing to report. A "no issues" email trains people to delete without reading, and then the one that mattered is lost too.
  • Anyone can turn theirs off, per category. If CR turns theirs off the panel still holds everything — nothing is lost, it is simply not pushed at them.
